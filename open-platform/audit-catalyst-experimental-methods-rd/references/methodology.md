# Audit methodology

This skill treats the input as a catalyst preparation and evaluation method, not necessarily a formal project proposal. A real submission may be a short procedure, a paper's Methods section, a patent example, or a machine-generated experimental route.

Review priorities are:

1. Can a trained researcher execute it safely with the stated information?
2. Can another laboratory reproduce it?
3. Can samples be compared fairly under controlled conditions?
4. Can the observed effect be attributed to the intended variable?
5. Can the planned evidence support each performance or mechanistic claim?

The audit therefore emphasizes operations, variables, controls, measurement traceability, uncertainty, and claim-to-test linkage. It does not validate results, certify safety, determine patentability, or replace discipline-specific review. Hazard analysis, pressure equipment, pyrophoric materials, toxic gases, waste, and institutional EHS requirements remain the user's responsibility.
