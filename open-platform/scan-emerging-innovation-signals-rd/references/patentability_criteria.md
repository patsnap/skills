# Screening and Technical-Evidence Criteria

Use this reference for early triage only. It does not establish patentability, eligibility, inventive step/non-obviousness, enablement, claim scope, validity, infringement, or freedom to operate.

## Keep two dimensions independent

### Dimension A: prior-art screening signal

Rate the result as:

- **Promising:** no close disclosure was found in the documented search, while material coverage limits remain.
- **Mixed:** related disclosures exist, but the reviewed records do not clearly disclose the differentiating combination.
- **Crowded:** many relevant disclosures or one apparently close disclosure warrant specialist review.
- **Not searched:** no reproducible external search was completed.

Do not use provider similarity scores, hit counts, rank position, or keyword frequency as a novelty test. They may help prioritize review but are not legal standards.

For every rating record:

- search date and database;
- query and strategy;
- jurisdictions and date coverage;
- classification and language coverage;
- result limit and returned count;
- publication or family unit;
- deduplication method;
- records actually reviewed;
- differentiating features checked;
- known coverage gaps;
- analyst confidence.

### Dimension B: technical evidence completeness

Rate the submitted material as:

- **Substantiated:** problem, implementation, effect, baseline, and supporting evidence are sufficiently specific for an IP professional to begin structured review.
- **Usable with gaps:** the core implementation is clear, but one or more boundaries, alternatives, results, contributor facts, or disclosure facts require follow-up.
- **Fragmentary:** the technical change cannot yet be reproduced or meaningfully searched.
- **Not assessable:** the material is non-technical, inaccessible, or outside the authorized scope.

Completeness is not claim readiness and does not guarantee enablement or written-description support.

## Evidence checklist

| Area | Strong evidence | Gap signal |
|---|---|---|
| Technical problem | Defined baseline and technical limitation | Business objective only |
| Implementation | Components, steps, relationships, conditions, or ranges | Generic outcome language |
| Effect | Test method, baseline, values, units, and uncertainty | Unsupported adjective |
| Alternatives | Variants, rejected routes, and boundary conditions | One unexplained embodiment |
| Reproducibility | Repeats, controls, samples, environment | Single undocumented observation |
| Contributors | Contribution facts by person | Attendance or authorship only |
| Disclosure | Event, date, audience, controls, content | Vague statement that it was shared |
| Search | Reproducible method and reviewed records | Undocumented result count |

## Confidence

Assign high, medium, or low confidence separately to extraction, search interpretation, and recommended action. Explain:

- source directness;
- document completeness;
- search breadth and recall risk;
- family/entity normalization;
- specialist-review depth;
- contradictory evidence;
- jurisdiction uncertainty.

## Action-priority matrix

Do not derive priority from two labels alone. Consider:

- disclosure urgency;
- technical and commercial significance;
- reversibility of the protection decision;
- evidence strength;
- maturity and reproducibility;
- search signal and coverage;
- ownership and confidentiality;
- strategic fit and available review capacity.

Recommended labels:

- **P1 — time-sensitive specialist review:** credible technical contribution plus a dated disclosure or decision trigger.
- **P2 — planned review:** actionable contribution without an immediate verified deadline.
- **P3 — evidence collection or monitoring:** insufficient evidence or low current strategic relevance.
- **Archive:** non-technical, duplicate, already fully disclosed without an identified path, or unsupported after review; retain rationale.

Never use color alone to convey priority.

## Interpretation examples

### No close result

Write: `No close disclosure was identified among the reviewed results under the documented query and cutoff. Screening signal: promising; systematic search still required.`

Do not write: `The invention is novel.`

### Close-looking patent record

Write: `Record E-014 appears to disclose features A and B; feature C requires claim/specification review by an IP professional.`

Do not write: `This patent blocks the concept` or `we infringe`.

### Missing performance data

Write: `The implementation is specific enough to search, but the stated effect is expected rather than demonstrated. Collect comparison data.`

Do not automatically discard the candidate.

## Output contract

For each candidate show:

1. screening signal;
2. screening basis and search ID;
3. screening confidence and limitations;
4. technical evidence completeness;
5. missing evidence;
6. action priority;
7. action rationale;
8. required specialist review.
