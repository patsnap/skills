# FTO Report Quality Standards

Version: 9.0 localized international edition

## 1. Scope and interpretation

These standards support the quality review of an existing freedom-to-operate
(FTO), patent-infringement-risk, or event IP risk report. They assess the
quality of the work product; they do not determine infringement, validity, or
freedom to operate.

Apply each criterion to the stated:

- product, process, service, or technical implementation;
- configuration and version;
- commercial activity;
- jurisdiction;
- claim version and translation;
- evidence and legal-status date;
- intended decision and audience.

No fixed number of databases, universal lookback period, universal report
validity period, or percentage threshold can replace that context.

## 2. Governing principles

### 2.1 Territoriality

Patent rights and infringement standards are territorial. A global family is a
research construct, not a single enforceable right. Review the appropriate
national or regional member, its current claims, ownership, and legal status.

### 2.2 Claim-centered analysis

Titles, abstracts, drawings, descriptions, classifications, and technical
similarity help identify candidates. Material infringement conclusions require
analysis of the relevant claim limitations against the defined product or
process evidence.

### 2.3 Evidence-date integrity

Legal status, ownership, claim scope, prosecution, product design, and business
activity can change. Every material statement must identify the evidence date
or state that the date is unavailable.

### 2.4 Reproducibility

A qualified reviewer should be able to reconstruct material searches,
screening, family consolidation, claim selection, and product mapping from the
retained record.

### 2.5 Qualified uncertainty

FTO searching has blind spots, including publication delay, database coverage,
translation, family normalization, indexing, and evolving claims. A good report
explains residual uncertainty rather than converting absence of evidence into
certainty.

### 2.6 Decision usefulness

The report must connect legal and technical findings to a named decision,
responsible owner, timing, trigger, and practical response.

## 3. Three-layer quality model

### 3.1 Layer 1 — Evidence and reproducibility

High-quality evidence work has:

- an unambiguous target subject and version;
- identified jurisdictions and commercial acts;
- named databases and authoritative sources;
- reproducible queries, fields, filters, and dates;
- explicit screening and exclusion rules;
- reconciled result and family counts;
- cited claim, status, ownership, and product evidence;
- visible limitations and unsearched areas.

Weak evidence work has:

- search descriptions without runnable queries;
- unexplained result counts;
- title-only or abstract-only classification;
- undated legal status;
- family members treated as interchangeable;
- conclusions that cannot be traced to source evidence.

### 3.2 Layer 2 — Legal reasoning

High-quality legal reasoning:

- identifies the applicable jurisdiction and activity;
- uses the current relevant claim set;
- states the claim-construction approach where material;
- maps every limitation to dated product/process evidence;
- separates literal infringement from equivalents or analogous doctrines;
- distinguishes infringement, validity, enforceability, ownership, and
  commercial materiality;
- qualifies pending applications and future prosecution;
- cites jurisdiction-specific authority where the conclusion depends on it.

Weak legal reasoning:

- assumes one country's doctrine applies globally;
- treats a family abstract as a claim chart;
- relies on expired, lapsed, cancelled, or superseded claims;
- treats a validity concern as automatic non-infringement;
- provides categorical assurances despite material missing evidence.

### 3.3 Layer 3 — Decision usefulness

High-quality decision support:

- states the decision and deadline;
- ranks issues by legal significance, commercial materiality, and confidence;
- identifies critical assumptions and decision gates;
- links higher-risk findings to concrete response options;
- names owners, timing, triggers, dependencies, and residual risk;
- defines monitoring and re-review events;
- communicates clearly to legal, IP, R&D, product, and management audiences.

Weak decision support lists patents without explaining what the business should
do, by when, or what remains uncertain.

## 4. Four-dimension scoring standard

### 4.1 Dimension A — Search-strategy quality (25 points)

#### A1. Source and jurisdiction coverage (8 points)

Evaluate whether the selected sources fit the target countries, right types,
technology, language, and decision. Do not use a fixed database-count grade.

Strong evidence:

- target-jurisdiction collections are covered;
- PCT/regional and national stages are reconciled when relevant;
- official registers support material legal-status findings;
- commercial/global tools retain source provenance;
- update lag and source limitations are disclosed.

#### A2. Query and route design (6 points)

Evaluate feature decomposition, terminology, translations, classifications,
semantic search, exact Boolean/proximity logic, entity variants, citations,
fields, filters, and iteration history.

A route is not independent merely because it uses a different interface. State
the retrieval mechanism and dependence between routes.

#### A3. Time and prosecution coverage (5 points)

Evaluate whether the time window reflects patent term, priority, technology
history, publication delay, prosecution, and the decision date. The Chinese
source's fixed 20-year and freshness values are not universal standards.

#### A4. Reproducibility and screening (6 points)

Evaluate whether searches can be rerun and whether raw, screened, retained, and
family counts reconcile under documented inclusion, exclusion, deduplication,
and family rules.

### 4.2 Dimension B — Patent-analysis depth (30 points)

#### B1. Claim comparison (10 points)

The report should identify the jurisdictional member, current claims, claim
version, source language, translation basis, and product/process version. Each
material limitation should map to evidence; gaps and interpretive uncertainty
must remain visible.

Field-specific rigor may include:

- chemical composition ranges, impurities, polymorphs, salts, and processes;
- biological sequences, identity thresholds, function, host, and use;
- mechanical relationships, geometry, tolerances, and component interaction;
- software functions, data flow, actor, execution location, and system claim;
- semiconductor layers, process steps, topology, materials, and packaging;
- standards clauses and implementation evidence for a genuine SEP question.

#### B2. Infringement-risk reasoning (8 points)

Assess the relevant act and actor, literal mapping, claim construction,
equivalents where applicable, indirect or divided infringement where factually
relevant, and jurisdiction-specific limitations. A risk label must state its
basis and confidence.

#### B3. Risk tiering (4 points)

Risk tiers must have written criteria and text labels. They should reflect at
least claim fit, legal status, jurisdiction, activity, evidence confidence, and
commercial materiality. Do not rely on color alone.

#### B4. Status, ownership, and validity context (5 points)

Material status and ownership require dated sources. Review expiry, lapse,
maintenance, extension/adjustment, opposition, reexamination, post-grant
proceedings, prosecution, litigation, disclaimers, and assignments only to the
degree relevant to the decision.

Validity research can change risk strategy but does not erase an enforceable
claim without a legally effective event.

#### B5. Industry-specific coverage (3 points)

Apply only justified field-specific checks. `N/A` is acceptable with a reason.
Do not import an automotive, pharmaceutical, software, standards, or exhibition
checklist into an unrelated report.

### 4.3 Dimension C — Legal-opinion quality (25 points)

#### C1. Legal framework (7 points)

The report should identify applicable law and cite current authority for
material propositions. Territoriality, claim interpretation, equivalents,
indirect infringement, exhaustion, licence, repair, safe harbour, experimental
use, importation, customs, and other doctrines are included only when relevant.

#### C2. Conclusion reliability (7 points)

Conclusions must follow from evidence, acknowledge contrary evidence and
uncertainty, and stay within the defined scope. Absolute statements such as
“zero risk,” “all patents found,” or “guaranteed FTO” fail this standard.

#### C3. Risk mitigation (6 points)

Material findings require claim-specific and decision-specific actions. Options
may include design-around, clarification of product facts, further status or
file-history review, targeted prior-art work, counsel opinion, licence,
acquisition, challenge, supplier allocation, indemnity review, launch hold,
monitoring, or risk acceptance by an authorized decision-maker.

#### C4. Professional communication (5 points)

Identify author and reviewer roles. Distinguish fact, assumption, analysis,
conclusion, and recommendation. Use terminology customary in the relevant
jurisdiction and sector, not unexplained internal acronyms.

### 4.4 Dimension D — Documentation completeness (20 points)

#### D1. Search record (6 points)

Retain databases, queries, fields, filters, dates, result counts, screening,
deduplication, family mapping, and route provenance.

#### D2. Analysis traceability (5 points)

Link each material conclusion to the correct patent identifier, claim text,
translation, status source/date, product evidence, and reviewer judgment.

#### D3. Evidence package (4 points)

Include or reference the search log, result list, family map, material claim
charts, patent extracts, register evidence, prosecution materials, and product
evidence needed for the intended reliance.

#### D4. Version and format control (5 points)

Record title, version, date, author, reviewer, approval, change history,
superseded versions, confidentiality, privilege, distribution, and retention.
Use accessible headings, captions, tables, labels, links, and print layout.

## 5. Grade interpretation

| Score | Grade | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 90–100 | Excellent | Strong report-quality evidence; residual issues remain bounded |
| 80–89 | Good | Generally reliable with defined improvements |
| 70–79 | Adequate | Conditionally usable within explicit limits |
| 60–69 | Needs improvement | Material gaps limit the intended reliance |
| Below 60 | Unsatisfactory | Insufficient for the stated decision |
| Any score with a fatal defect | Fatal | Do not rely on the report until cured |

The bands describe report quality, not infringement probability. A high score
does not guarantee FTO; a low score does not prove infringement.

## 6. Fatal-defect standard

Fatal defects are non-compensable failures of subject, jurisdiction, evidence,
claim-level basis, material completeness, or conclusion qualification. When
triggered:

1. display the defect above the score;
2. assign grade `Fatal`;
3. retain diagnostic scores;
4. complete the assessment;
5. state the cure required before reliance.

## 7. Independent-search quality

### 7.1 Required route record

For each applicable route record:

- purpose and technical concepts;
- provider/database and fields;
- exact query or reproducible request;
- filters and date run;
- raw and retained counts;
- normalized publication and family identifiers;
- route provenance and limitations.

### 7.2 Comparison units

State whether comparisons use publications, applications, grants, simple
families, extended families, or a custom family rule. Do not mix units in a
single denominator.

### 7.3 Omission classification

Classify differences as identifier/family differences, relevant non-material
additions, potentially material omissions, or confirmed material omissions.
Only the latter two require claim/status investigation; only supported findings
should affect the score.

### 7.4 Recall and coverage language

Observed route union and overlap are empirical. True recall requires knowledge
of the unobserved relevant set and ordinarily cannot be established from
correlated patent searches.

A Chapman, capture-recapture, Jackknife, or related estimate is acceptable only
as a qualified heuristic with defined pools, unit, assumptions, equation,
uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations. Otherwise state `Not estimated`.

## 8. Pending applications and monitoring

Pending claims can change and are not currently enforceable patent claims.
Maintain a separate watchlist containing:

- application/publication and family identifiers;
- jurisdiction and current procedural state;
- claims or features to monitor;
- expected or observed event;
- trigger and owner;
- review cadence based on prosecution and launch timing.

Do not apply a universal 12-month validity or 18-month staleness rule. Define
re-review based on material changes, including:

- product or process redesign;
- new target country or commercial act;
- claim amendment, grant, opposition, reexamination, or litigation;
- ownership or licence change;
- new relevant publication;
- material database/status correction;
- approaching launch, transaction, event, or decision gate.

## 9. SEP/FRAND quality standard

Apply only when an identified standard and implementation are in scope.

Review:

- asserted patent and jurisdictional member;
- current claims and status;
- declared standard and version;
- claim-to-standard and implementation mapping;
- declaration database limits;
- essentiality assessment source and date;
- licensing, pool, and litigation context where relevant;
- jurisdiction-specific FRAND or competition-law issues.

A declaration is not proof of essentiality, validity, infringement, or an
available licence on any assumed terms.

## 10. International-market localization standard

An internationally suitable report should:

- use English professional terminology and define unavoidable abbreviations;
- use ISO dates or unambiguous written dates;
- distinguish publication, application, and grant numbers;
- identify currency and tax basis for financial figures;
- identify jurisdiction for every legal proposition;
- avoid China-only administrative categories unless the target market requires
  them and an explanation is supplied;
- link global PatSnap resources through `open.patsnap.com`;
- retain the Chinese marketplace URL only in the migration index, not the
  distributed skill;
- use accessible scientific/legal visual design.

## 11. Evidence-source hierarchy

Prefer evidence appropriate to the proposition:

1. official patent office register, file wrapper, court, statute, regulation,
   standards body, or other primary authority;
2. PatSnap and other reputable normalized patent-data providers;
3. issuer, assignee, or counterparty records;
4. qualified secondary legal or technical sources;
5. analyst inference, explicitly labeled and supported.

Normalized databases improve research efficiency but may require confirmation
against the authoritative source for decision-material facts.

## 12. Output quality standard

The final assessment must include:

- identity, scope, scenario, and limitations;
- fatal-defect state;
- executive summary;
- three-layer findings;
- four-dimension scores and deductions;
- independent-search comparison and omissions;
- claim-analysis and risk lists;
- response-measure assessment and recommendations;
- consolidated issue and remediation registers;
- decision suitability, re-review triggers, and disclaimer.

Use semantic HTML, fixed CSS, text labels, table captions, accessible contrast,
print support, safe links, escaped dynamic content, and no inline styles. Avoid
decorative charts that imply unsupported measurement precision.

## 13. Minimum evidence for a “Pass”

A criterion passes only when the evidence is:

- present;
- relevant to the defined scope;
- identifiable and dated where time matters;
- internally consistent;
- reproducible or independently inspectable;
- sufficiently current for the decision;
- no more certain than the source supports.

Presence of a heading, generic boilerplate, or an uncited assertion is not a
pass.

## 14. Disclaimer

These standards support report-quality assurance. They are not legal advice,
do not create a legal opinion, and do not guarantee freedom to operate. Patent
claims, ownership, status, product facts, and law can change. Qualified counsel
should review material conclusions for the applicable jurisdiction and decision.
